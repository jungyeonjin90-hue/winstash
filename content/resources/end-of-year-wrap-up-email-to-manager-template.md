As December approaches, the workplace enters a familiar phase: people scramble to finish end-of-year review forms, close out Q4 deliverables, and head out on holiday PTO.

Most employees simply log off for the holidays with an automatic out-of-office message.

Top performers do something smarter: they send a concise, thoughtful **End-of-Year Wrap-Up Email** to their manager.

This email takes ten minutes to write, but it does three high-value things:
1. It cements your top achievements in your manager's mind as annual reviews and planning wrap up at many companies.
2. It gives your manager peace of mind that all holiday coverage and urgent handoffs are handled.
3. It sets a clear, ambitious tone for your priorities heading into Q1.

Below is a copy-and-paste email template, filled-in examples for different roles, and tips on timing.

## The copy-and-paste year-end wrap-up email template

Keep this short enough to read on a mobile screen in 60 seconds:

```
Subject: Year-end recap & holiday coverage — [Your Name]

Hi [Manager Name],

As we wrap up the year and head into the holidays, I wanted to send a quick recap of what we accomplished, our holiday coverage plan, and where we're heading in Q1.

Top Highlights from [Year]:
- [Outcome 1]: [Big project or rollout] shipped on schedule, resulting in [before/after impact or metric].
- [Outcome 2]: Replaced [manual bottleneck/process] with [new checklist/system], cutting [approval time / errors / manual steps].
- [Outcome 3]: [Team multiplier: onboarding, mentoring, documentation, or cross-team unblocking].

Holiday Coverage & Loose Ends:
- I'll be out on PTO from [Start Date] to [End Date], returning on [Return Date].
- [Teammate Name] is covering my emergency queue while I'm away; all open project docs and handoff notes are linked in our shared channel.
- If an urgent Sev-1 emergency arises that only I can answer, feel free to text or call my cell at [Phone Number].

Looking Ahead to Q1:
- [Priority 1 for next quarter tied to team roadmap]
- [Priority 2 for next quarter]

Thank you for your support, guidance, and partnership throughout [Year]—I'm really proud of what our team delivered and excited for what's ahead.

Have a wonderful and restful holiday break!

Best,
[Your Name]
```

## Two filled-in year-end email examples

These are illustrative examples. Replace the details with your own work and only use numbers you can back up.

### Example 1: Operations / Project Management

```
Subject: Year-end recap & holiday coverage — Alex

Hi Rachel,

As we head into the holiday break, I wanted to share a quick summary of our team's operational wins this year and our coverage plan for the freeze.

Top Highlights from 2026:
- Overhauled our vendor approval workflow: moved requests from three email inboxes into one shared intake tracker, cutting average approval turnaround from ~6 days to ~2 days.
- Month-end close reliability: built our standardized close checklist with finance, going from three late closes last year to zero this year.
- ERP rollout governance: led cross-functional risk tracking with legal and IT, identifying a compliance gap two months early and delivering on schedule.

Holiday Coverage:
- I will be out on PTO from Dec 21 to Jan 4.
- Jordan is covering urgent vendor escalations; all runbooks are pinned in #ops-internal.
- For true emergencies, you can reach me at 555-0192.

Q1 Focus:
- Automate contract renewal reminders 60 days ahead of cutoff dates.
- Standardize our quarterly kickoff template across all new departmental projects.

Thanks so much for your mentorship and air cover this year—working together has been a highlight of my year. Enjoy the well-deserved time off with your family!

Best,
Alex
```

### Example 2: Software Engineering / Technical Roles

```
Subject: Year-end recap & holiday coverage — Marcus

Hi David,

Before logging off for the holiday freeze, I wanted to send a quick recap of what we shipped and confirm our on-call coverage plan.

Key Engineering Highlights from 2026:
- Core service migration: led the zero-downtime database cutover in Q3 with p99 latency remaining under 45ms at peak traffic.
- Reliability & tech debt: traced and patched the nightly export timezone bug, eliminating manual on-call reruns and bringing weekly job failures to zero.
- Infrastructure & resilience: resolved a database connection pool leak in staging, eliminating pod crash loops during high-concurrency batch jobs.

Holiday Coverage & Code Freeze:
- I am on PTO from Dec 23 through Jan 3.
- All scheduled feature branches are merged and verified in staging; no releases are scheduled during the freeze.
- Priya is primary on-call for Week 1; my cell is 555-0144 if an unrecoverable infrastructure issue arises.

Q1 Priorities:
- Break down the monolithic auth middleware into decoupled modules.
- Onboard our two incoming Q1 engineering hires using our updated local setup runbook.

Thank you for your trust and guidance throughout this year's technical challenges. Have a fantastic holiday break and happy New Year!

Cheers,
Marcus
```

## Why this email leaves a lasting impression

Managers have to evaluate dozens of moving parts in December. A clear email that celebrates wins and ties up loose ends demonstrates senior professionalism:

1. **It reinforces your annual review packet:** At companies where review calibrations continue into December, this email provides a timely, positive reminder of your high-leverage work.
2. **It prevents holiday anxiety:** Your manager doesn't have to wonder who is covering the queue or if a project will stall while you're skiing.
3. **It demonstrates ownership:** You are already thinking about Q1 priorities before January even starts.
