If you work in Customer Support, Technical Support, or Help Desk Operations, you solve dozens of problems every week. The catch is that individual tickets blur together fast. By the time performance review season arrives, it's hard to remember anything beyond *"I answered tickets quickly and kept customers happy."*

Managers already see your basic ticket count on a dashboard. What they don't always see—and what gets you a top rating or promotion—is **how you reduced repeat tickets, de-escalated frustrated customers, spotted product bugs early, and made the rest of the support team faster.**

Below are before-and-after rewrites, copy-and-paste examples across five core support areas, and a fill-in template. Replace every detail with your own work and only use numbers you can back up.

## Before and after: showing impact beyond the ticket queue

| Weak (generic duty) | Strong (shows deflection, escalation skill, or team impact) |
| --- | --- |
| I answered customer tickets quickly and kept CSAT high. | Handled [N]+ tickets per week while maintaining [X]% CSAT, and cleared our Monday morning backlog ahead of SLA throughout Q3. |
| I helped frustrated customers. | De-escalated [N] billing and outage threads from at-risk accounts by diagnosing the root cause with engineering and following up until their workflows were restored. |
| I wrote some help center articles. | Noticed the same SSO setup question coming up every week, wrote a step-by-step help guide with screenshots, and created a saved reply so the team could answer in one touch. |
| I reported bugs to the engineering team. | Spotted a pattern of failed CSV imports after the June release, documented clean reproduction steps in Jira, and helped engineering ship a same-day hotfix. |

## Self-evaluation examples by Customer Support area

### 1. Queue ownership, quality, and SLA reliability

- "Maintained a [X]% CSAT score across [N]+ interactions this period while meeting first-response and resolution SLAs in our tier-1 and tier-2 queues."
- "Stepped into the billing and permissions queue during our Q3 seasonal spike, clearing [N] aged tickets without dropping response quality."
- "Improved first-contact resolution on setup tickets by asking for configuration logs up front and replying with complete, step-by-step checklists."

### 2. Escalations and technical troubleshooting

- "Served as the escalation point for complex integration and API issues, resolving [majority / N] of escalated tickets without needing engineering intervention."
- "Calmed an urgent escalation from an enterprise customer during the [Month] export incident by hopping on a live screen-share, providing a safe workaround, and updating them until the permanent fix went live."
- "Investigated a recurring permissions error that had stumped three previous threads, identified the conflicting role setting, and shared the fix in our team channel."

### 3. Help center docs, macros, and ticket deflection

- "Wrote [N] new help center articles and updated [M] outdated screenshots ahead of the v2 UI launch, so customers and reps had accurate self-serve guides on day one."
- "Audited our shared saved replies (macros), archiving outdated templates and writing five new macros for our most common billing and login questions."
- "Created an internal troubleshooting decision tree for SSO and webhook errors that reduced escalations from tier-1 to tier-2."

### 4. Product feedback and bug reporting (Voice of the Customer)

- "Caught and escalated a checkout / login bug within 20 minutes of the first three tickets arriving, giving engineering exact reproduction steps and browser logs to cut debugging time."
- "Tagged and summarized the top five recurring customer friction points each month and shared them with Product, directly informing the Q3 export improvements."
- "Tested upcoming features in staging before release and flagged two confusing error messages so design could fix them before customers saw them."

### 5. Teamwork, onboarding, and shift handoffs

- "Acted as onboarding buddy for two new support specialists, reviewing their draft replies in week one and helping both ramp to full queue ownership by week three."
- "Left clear, reproducible notes on all open multi-day tickets before shift handoffs and PTO so teammates never had to ask a customer to repeat themselves."
- "Answered daily troubleshooting questions from peers in our support help channel, helping newer reps resolve tricky edge cases faster."

## Honest areas for improvement examples for support roles

- "Earlier in the year, I sometimes spent too long trying to reproduce an obscure technical bug solo before escalating to tier-2 or engineering. I now follow a 30-minute timebox before posting what I've tested and looping in a specialist."
- "When the queue got busy in Q2, I answered recurring questions one by one instead of pausing to update our saved reply. In H2, I blocked 30 minutes every Friday to turn any new repeat question into a shared macro or help doc."

## Fill-in template for Customer & Technical Support

```
Role: [Customer Support Specialist / Technical Support Engineer / Support Lead]
Review period: [Start] to [End]

1. Queue performance & reliability:
- Handled [volume / queue area] at [CSAT / SLA metric or consistent quality], stepping in during [peak period or coverage gap].

2. Escalations & complex troubleshooting:
- Resolved [type of complex issue or escalation] by [how you diagnosed or de-escalated it], resulting in [saved account / faster fix].

3. Documentation & ticket deflection:
- Created/updated [N help articles, macros, or internal runbooks] for [topic], making it faster for [customers / teammates] to solve [problem].

4. Product & bug feedback:
- Identified [bug or recurring customer friction], provided [repro steps / ticket summary] to [product/engineering], leading to [fix or improvement].

5. Goals for next period:
- [1-3 concrete goals—see our performance review goals guide]
```
