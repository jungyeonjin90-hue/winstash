When you sit down to write your self-evaluation or update your resume, two frameworks come up more than any others: **Google's XYZ formula** and the **STAR method**.

People often ask which one they should use. The short answer is that they solve two different length problems:

- **The XYZ formula** compresses a win into **one punchy bullet point** (15–30 words).
- **The STAR method** unpacks a complex win into **a short, checkable story** (3–5 sentences).

Below is how each formula works, side-by-side examples of the exact same work written both ways, and how to combine them in your next performance review without inventing fake numbers.

## Quick comparison: XYZ formula vs. STAR method

| | XYZ Formula | STAR Method |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Structure** | Accomplished **[X]** as measured by **[Y]**, by doing **[Z]** | **S**ituation, **T**ask, **A**ction, **R**esult |
| **Ideal length** | 1 sentence (a single bullet point) | 3–5 sentences (a short paragraph or 4 bullets) |
| **Best used for** | Brag sheets, resumes, weekly updates, and secondary review wins | Top 2–3 flagship review examples, promotion packets, and interviews |
| **Main strength** | Easy to skim in five seconds | Shows obstacles, trade-offs, and your specific ownership |

## Side-by-side examples: the same work in XYZ vs. STAR

Here is how the exact same real-world work looks in both formats across four different roles. Notice how neither version invents a fake percentage.

### 1. Operations: fixing a slow approval workflow

**Written as an XYZ bullet (for a resume or summary list):**
> Cut vendor invoice approval time from ~6 days to ~2 days (**X & Y**) by replacing three separate email inboxes with one shared intake sheet and a weekly finance triage (**Z**).

**Written as a STAR story (for a deep-dive self-evaluation question):**
> - **Situation:** Vendor invoices needed sign-off from three teams and frequently stalled in scattered email threads ahead of month-end close.
> - **Task:** I took ownership of making approvals predictable before Q3 close.
> - **Action:** I mapped where requests got stuck, built a single shared intake sheet with clear owners and due dates, and ran a 15-minute weekly triage with finance and procurement.
> - **Result:** Finance stopped chasing approvals by email, and average turnaround time dropped from roughly six days to two days.

### 2. Project / Product Management: navigating a late change

**Written as an XYZ bullet:**
> Shipped the Q3 pricing page relaunch on the original target date (**X**) with zero post-launch compliance issues (**Y**) by re-sequencing design reviews and running daily 15-minute check-ins after late legal copy changes (**Z**).

**Written as a STAR story:**
> - **Situation:** Two weeks before our planned pricing page relaunch, legal requested copy changes across the comparison table.
> - **Task:** Keep our committed launch date without skipping compliance sign-off.
> - **Action:** I re-sequenced the remaining design work, set up a daily 15-minute check-in for the final two weeks, and had legal review each section as it finished instead of waiting for one final pass.
> - **Result:** We launched on the planned date with full legal approval and no last-minute fire drills.

### 3. Finance / Accounting: preventing late month-end closes

**Written as an XYZ bullet:**
> Eliminated late month-end closes (**X**), going from three late closes last year to zero this year (**Y**), by building a standardized close checklist with cutoff reminders and backup owners (**Z**).

**Written as a STAR story:**
> - **Situation:** Last year our team missed the month-end close deadline three times when key reconciliations waited on late departmental handoffs.
> - **Task:** Make our monthly close predictable and remove single-person bottlenecks.
> - **Action:** I built a shared close checklist with explicit cutoff dates for each department, automated our two slowest reconciliation templates, and cross-trained a backup reviewer.
> - **Result:** We completed every month-end close on schedule this year—going from three late closes to zero—even during team PTO.

### 4. Engineering / Support: stopping a recurring failure

**Written as an XYZ bullet:**
> Eliminated manual reruns of the nightly customer export job (**X & Y**) by tracing intermittent failures to a timezone filter bug, shipping the fix, and adding a regression test (**Z**).

**Written as a STAR story:**
> - **Situation:** Our nightly export job failed two or three times a week, forcing whoever was on call to rerun it by hand in the morning.
> - **Task:** Find the root cause and stop the recurring manual work.
> - **Action:** I audited the failure logs, traced the issue to a timezone boundary bug in the date filter, patched the query, and added an automated regression test.
> - **Result:** The export job has run cleanly every night since the fix with zero manual reruns.

## How to use XYZ when you don't have a hard metric for "Y"

A common frustration with *"Accomplished X as measured by Y, by doing Z"* is feeling stuck when you don't have a revenue or conversion percentage for **Y**.

In non-sales roles, **Y** doesn't have to be a percentage. Use a concrete operational change instead:

- **Before vs. after time:** "...as measured by cutting setup time from three weeks to eight days, by..."
- **Before vs. after count:** "...reducing stuck contract requests from 40 to 0 in six weeks, by..."
- **Adoption proof:** "...adopted by all 15 stakeholders as the standard department template, by..."
- **Zero-defect proof:** "...passing the annual audit with zero findings, by..."

## How to combine both in your self-evaluation form

When you fill out your performance review, don't write ten long STAR paragraphs (your manager will get tired of reading) and don't write only one-line bullets (your biggest wins will lack context). Combine them like this:

```
Top Accomplishments — [Review Period]

Flagship Outcomes (2-3 STAR stories for your biggest projects):
1. [Project / Initiative Name]
   - Situation & Task: [1 sentence on the problem and your goal]
   - Action: [1-2 sentences on what YOU specifically did]
   - Result: [1 sentence on what changed, with real numbers or before/after proof]

2. [Second Flagship Project]
   - ...

Additional Wins & Operational Impact (3-5 XYZ bullets):
- [Outcome X] ([proof or before/after Y]) by [specific action Z].
- [Outcome X] ([proof or before/after Y]) by [specific action Z].
- [Outcome X] ([proof or before/after Y]) by [specific action Z].
```
