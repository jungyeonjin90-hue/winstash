Every performance review cycle ends with a quiet prompt at the bottom of the portal: **"Employee Comments / Response"**.

Whether you received an *"Exceeds Expectations"* rating, a mixed review with unexpected criticism, or a rating that feels unfair, what you write in that box—and what you email afterward—stays in your career record.

Leaving it blank wastes an opportunity to lock in next-year momentum. Writing an emotional rebuttal hurts your credibility with HR and future managers. The strongest response is **composed, specific, and forward-looking.**

## Before and after: responding to tough or incomplete feedback

These are illustrative examples. Replace the details with your own work and only use numbers you can back up.

| Weak (defensive or passive) | Strong (composed, factual, constructive) |
| --- | --- |
| Agreed. Thanks. | Appreciate the clear feedback and recognition on the Q3 migration. My priority next quarter is partnering earlier with legal on launch specs and aligning on milestones toward [Next Level]. |
| This review is completely unfair because you forgot I ran the entire billing redesign and nobody ever told me my updates were bad. | Thank you for sharing this perspective. For completeness in my file, I also wanted to note the Q2 billing redesign shipped on schedule with support and finance. Looking ahead, I'd love to establish a bi-weekly status check so expectations stay aligned early. |
| I don't think my communication was an issue—other teams just didn't read my Slack messages. | Thank you for raising stakeholder visibility as a focus area. While I shared updates in team channels, I see how a concise weekly summary doc or email would make decisions easier for partner leads to follow. I'm implementing that starting next week. |
| Okay, I'll try to be more strategic next year. | Thank you for coaching me toward broader scope this year. In Q1, I plan to focus on leading the [Initiative] kickoff memo and proposing clear priority trade-offs before execution starts. |

## Copy-and-paste performance review responses by scenario

### 1. Responding to a strong / positive performance review

Use the comment box to reinforce your top wins and point clearly toward your next step (whether promotion, raise readiness, or a stretch role).

```
Thank you for the thoughtful review and support throughout the year. I'm especially proud of how our team delivered [Project A] and streamlined [Process B] together.

As we head into Q1, my focus is taking on broader ownership across [Next Area / Cross-Team Initiative] and building toward [Next Level / Goal]. I look forward to checking in on those milestones during our upcoming 1:1s.
```

### 2. Responding to mixed feedback (mostly good, one sharp critique)

Acknowledge the positive parts, accept the coaching without making excuses, and state one concrete change you will make.

```
Thank you for the balanced review and candid coaching. I appreciate the recognition on [Core Strength / Project Shipped], and I take the feedback on [Growth Area, e.g., surfacing schedule risks earlier] seriously.

Going forward, I'm going to [specific habit change, e.g., send a brief mid-sprint risk check every Wednesday and circulate rough drafts 48 hours earlier]. I'd appreciate checking back in after 60 days to make sure this shift meets what you're looking for.
```

### 3. Responding when major achievements were left out

Managers often pull reviews together quickly across many reports. Add factual context calmly so calibration readers and future managers see the full picture.

```
Thank you for taking the time to put this review together and discuss my growth this year. For completeness in my annual record, I'd also like to note two H1–H2 deliverables we didn't get to cover in full detail:
- [Project 1]: Led [scope] with [Partner Team], launching on [date/milestone].
- [Improvement 2]: Documented [onboarding/process guide], used by [team/new hires].

Looking ahead to next cycle, I'm excited to apply the feedback on [Focus Area] and track these priorities together in our weekly 1:1s.
```

### 4. Responding professionally to a review you believe is unfair or inaccurate

Keep emotion completely out of the written response. State verifiable facts neutrally and ask for specific, written expectations going forward.

```
I appreciate you walking through this evaluation with me. While we have different perspectives on a few items noted in [Section Name], I want to make sure my record reflects a couple of verifiable project facts:
- [Project/Task]: Delivered on [date] per the milestones outlined in [linked doc/plan].
- Feedback cadence: [Note respectfully if concerns were not raised prior to the final review, or clarify external blocker context factually].

Most importantly, I want to be fully aligned on expectations from here. As we discussed, I've summarized the specific 60-day deliverables we agreed on and welcome weekly check-ins so there are no surprises moving forward.
```

### 5. Follow-up email to request a constructive action plan (24–48 hours after meeting)

```
Subject: Follow-up & 60-day goals from my performance review

Hi [Manager Name],

Thanks again for our review conversation yesterday and for sharing direct feedback. After reflecting on our discussion, I put together a quick outline of what I'm focusing on over the next two months:

1. [Growth Area 1]: [Specific weekly behavior change]
2. [Delivery Milestone]: [Priority project or handoff target by Date]
3. Check-in cadence: Reviewing progress briefly during our first 1:1 of each month.

Please let me know if you'd adjust any part of this focus. I appreciate your guidance and want to make strong progress this quarter.

Best,
[Your Name]
```

## Four golden rules before clicking "Submit Response"

1. **Sleep on it for 24 hours**: Never submit employee comments right after reading a disappointing review. Draft in a private doc, reread calmly the next morning, and strip out adjectives or sarcasm.
2. **Remember your audience**: HR partners, skip-level directors, and future hiring managers may read this comment years later. Write so any neutral third party sees maturity and professionalism.
3. **Stick to verifiable facts**: Cite dates, project docs, and agreed milestones—never gossip about teammates or blame other departments emotionally.
4. **Lock in expectations early next year**: Keeping short weekly work notes makes it nearly impossible for key wins to be forgotten or misunderstood next cycle.
