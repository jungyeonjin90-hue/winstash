Leadership is one of the highest-weighted competencies in mid-level and senior performance reviews—even for individual contributors who manage zero direct reports.

Yet when people try to describe leadership in a self-evaluation, they often fall back on empty labels like *"I demonstrated strong leadership and was proactive throughout the year."* Reviewers quickly discount self-awarded adjectives unless there is proof behind them.

Real leadership at work shows up in **what moved because you stepped in**: ambiguity you clarified, a cross-team conflict you resolved, a junior teammate you leveled up, or an unowned risk you caught early.

## 20 performance review phrases for leadership & initiative

These are illustrative examples. Replace the details with your own work and only use numbers you can back up.

### 1. Initiative & stepping into unowned gaps

- "Identified an unowned handoff gap between [Team A] and [Team B], proposed a lightweight ownership matrix, and piloted it across two sprints before rolling it out team-wide."
- "Volunteered to tackle our oldest backlog of recurring customer friction points during Q2, organizing the issues into three themes and shipping fixes for the top category."
- "Flagged a compliance deadline risk six weeks early when no project owner was assigned, assembled the working checklist, and coordinated inputs from finance and ops to pass on schedule."
- "Built a starter troubleshooting playbook after handling our first major [tool/workflow] migration hiccup, turning a one-off firefight into a repeatable reference for the whole org."
- "Initiated a quarterly post-mortem review on slipped estimates, helping the team spot hidden external dependencies earlier during sprint scoping."

### 2. Aligning stakeholders & driving decisions

- "Facilitated a cross-functional alignment workshop between [Sales], [Product], and [Engineering] to resolve conflicting launch priorities, leaving with one signed-off milestone plan."
- "Translated ambiguous executive goals for [Initiative] into three concrete quarterly workstreams with explicit trade-offs so the team could execute without weekly re-approvals."
- "Wrote a clear decision memo comparing two vendor paths—laying out cost, migration effort, and risk—which enabled leadership to sign off in a single 30-minute review."
- "Kept five partner teams aligned during a high-visibility [Q3/Q4] rollout through concise Friday status notes highlighting blockers and exact owners needed."
- "Proactively pushed back on a late scope addition during [Project], presenting timeline trade-offs clearly to stakeholders so we protected core launch quality."

### 3. Mentoring, coaching & raising team standards

- "Designed our team's 30-60-90 day technical ramp checklist and served as onboarding mentor for [N] new hires, cutting repeat environment-setup questions."
- "Elevated peer feedback culture by modeling specific, constructive code/design reviews focused on user impact and maintainability rather than stylistic nitpicks."
- "Coached a teammate through writing and presenting their first project proposal to senior stakeholders, reviewing their dry run and helping sharpen the executive takeaway."
- "Hosted bi-weekly lunch-and-learn demos on [domain/tool] to break down knowledge silos so critical workflows never relied on a single point of failure."
- "Created a shared template library for [client proposals / QA plans / incident reviews], raising consistency across all new team deliverables."

### 4. Composure, trade-offs & accountability

- "Took direct accountability when [launch/deliverable] hit an unexpected snag, communicated the root cause transparently to stakeholders, and shipped the remediation within [timeframe]."
- "Protected team focus during a chaotic re-org by reprioritizing active commitments with my manager and deferring low-value tasks cleanly."
- "Stepped up as interim lead for [recurring meeting / triage queue] while our manager was away, keeping intake SLAs intact and surfacing only true escalations upon their return."
- "Balanced speed and safety on [Project] by scoping an incremental beta rollout with real customer guardrails rather than delaying months for a full-bang release."
- "Encouraged quieter team members during planning debates to share edge-case concerns, catching an integration flaw that improved the final design."

## Fill-in formula to write your own leadership bullets

Use this three-part pattern whenever you write about initiative or influence:

```
Leadership Bullet Formula:
[Unowned gap / ambiguity / conflict you spotted]
+ [Concrete leadership action: proposed plan / aligned teams / coached peer / made trade-off]
+ [Observable team or business change as a result]

Copy-and-paste template:
"Noticed [specific friction or risk] across [teams/workflow]. Took initiative to [created doc / aligned stakeholders / stepped in to lead X], which enabled [team/project] to [concrete result or prevented problem]."
```
