Writing a self-evaluation in finance, accounting, or FP&A has a built-in trap: when you do your job well, the numbers tie out, the books close on schedule, vendors get paid accurately, and the audit passes without drama.

Because leadership expects the numbers to be right, writing "Completed monthly reconciliations and prepared budget reports" can make a full year of hard work sound like routine maintenance.

A strong finance or accounting self-evaluation highlights **speed, accuracy, risk prevention, and decision support**: how many days you shaved off the close, what discrepancy you caught before a report went to the board, or how your model helped a department lead make a trade-off. Below are before-and-after rewrites, copy-and-paste examples across five finance areas, and a fill-in template.

## Before and after: from routine bookkeeper to trusted finance partner

| Weak (lists recurring duties) | Strong (shows close speed, controls, or decision impact) |
| --- | --- |
| I completed month-end close every month this year. | Built a shared month-end close checklist with clear owners and cutoff times, going from three late closes last year to zero this year. |
| I processed vendor invoices and handled AP questions. | Moved vendor invoice approvals from three email inboxes into one intake sheet with weekly triage, cutting average approval time from ~6 days to ~2 days. |
| I helped prepare the annual budget and quarterly forecasts. | Rebuilt the department headcount and software forecast model in one shared workbook, eliminating version mix-ups during Q4 budget reviews. |
| I supported the external audit. | Prepared all PBC audit schedules ahead of the deadline and standardized our revenue support documentation, completing the annual audit with zero adjustments. |

## Self-evaluation examples by finance & accounting area

Swap the details below with your own work, and only keep numbers you can verify.

### 1. Month-end close and accounting operations

- "Standardized the month-end close calendar across AP, payroll, and revenue accounting, reducing our close timeline from [X] business days to [Y] business days in H2."
- "Automated three recurring journal entry calculations and bank reconciliation templates, removing manual copy-paste steps and preventing formula errors."
- "Documented step-by-step runbooks for our top ten close tasks and cross-trained a backup reviewer so close stayed on schedule during team PTO."

### 2. Audit, tax, and internal controls

- "Led PBC preparation for the annual financial audit across [N] schedules, delivering all requested samples on time with zero audit adjustments or control deficiencies."
- "Tightened approval controls on corporate card and expense reimbursements by updating policy thresholds and setting up a monthly exception check."
- "Reconciled historical balance sheet clearing accounts and resolved [N] aged items prior to year-end close."

### 3. Budgeting, forecasting, and FP&A

- "Partnered with [ engineering / marketing / ops ] leads on monthly budget-vs-actual reviews, translating variance drivers into plain language so department heads could adjust Q3 spend early."
- "Built a vendor spend breakdown ahead of Q4 planning that identified [N] overlapping software subscriptions before their auto-renewal dates."
- "Created a standardized board-deck metrics tab with product and sales ops so monthly ARR, churn, and burn figures tied to the general ledger without last-minute reconciliation."

### 4. Accounts payable (AP), accounts receivable (AR), and billing

- "Cleaned up the customer billing and collections follow-up sequence with Customer Success, reducing overdue 60+ day invoices across my assigned portfolio."
- "Resolved a recurring billing proration discrepancy with engineering and ops before quarter-end invoicing, preventing customer disputes."
- "Consolidated regional vendor onboarding forms into one W-9 / banking verification workflow, stopping payment delays caused by missing tax forms."

### 5. Cross-functional partnership and process improvement

- "Ran a weekly 20-minute sync between finance, procurement, and legal that cleared [N] stuck contract and PO requests in six weeks."
- "Created a one-page 'how to submit a purchase request' guide for non-finance managers, cutting back-and-forth Slack questions on missing budget codes."
- "Supported the ERP / billing system migration by mapping our chart of accounts and validating parallel close numbers across two cycles before cutover."

## Honest areas for improvement examples for finance roles

- "Earlier in the year, my monthly variance emails to department leads included large tables without enough context on what action was needed. Starting in Q3, I added a three-bullet summary at the top highlighting the main driver and any decision needed."
- "During Q1 close, I spent too long troubleshooting a data import discrepancy solo before flagging it. I now alert the team as soon as a reconciliation dependency slips by more than half a day."

## Fill-in template for Finance & Accounting

```
Role: [Staff Accountant / Senior Accountant / Financial Analyst / FP&A / Controller]
Review period: [Start] to [End]

1. Close efficiency & accuracy:
- Owned [close area / reconciliations] across [N cycles], improved [checklist or template], resulting in [days saved / 0 late closes / fewer manual steps].

2. Audit, controls & compliance:
- Prepared/maintained [audit schedules / tax / internal controls], catching or preventing [specific discrepancy or risk] with [audit outcome].

3. Forecasting & business partnering:
- Partnered with [departments] on [budget / forecast / spend analysis], uncovering [insight or cost savings] that informed [decision].

4. Process & systems improvements:
- Simplified [AP / AR / procurement / reporting workflow] by [what you built], reducing [turnaround time or manual follow-ups].

5. Goals for next period:
- [1-3 concrete goals—see our performance review goals guide]
```
