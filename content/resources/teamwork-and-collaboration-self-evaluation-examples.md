Almost every company performance review has a competency section called **Teamwork**, **Collaboration**, or **Cross-Functional Partnership**.

And almost everyone fills it out the same way: *"I am always willing to help teammates and worked closely with engineering, product, and marketing this year."*

The trouble with "worked closely with" is that it describes proximity, not impact. Sitting in the same meetings as another team doesn't tell your manager what changed because you were in the room.

A strong collaboration answer names **the handoff or friction point between people**, **the specific part you played**, and **what moved forward as a result**. Below are before-and-after rewrites, 16 copy-and-paste examples, and a fill-in template.

## Before and after: replacing "worked closely with"

| Weak (describes proximity) | Strong (shows how you unblocked the work) |
| --- | --- |
| I worked closely with many teams this year. | Ran a weekly 20-minute sync between finance, procurement, and legal that cleared 40 stuck vendor requests in six weeks. |
| I am always happy to help my teammates. | Wrote our team's first-week onboarding checklist and paired with two new hires in Q2, who both started handling their own queue in week two. |
| I collaborated with engineering and design on launches. | Built a shared release checklist across product, design, and support so every launch this half went out with help docs and a sales note on day one. |
| I communicated well with stakeholders. | Sent a one-page Friday status update to 15 cross-functional stakeholders, which cut ad-hoc status check-ins and was adopted as the team template. |

## 16 teamwork and collaboration examples by scenario

Swap the details below with your own projects and teammates.

### 1. Cross-functional alignment and unblocking handoffs

- "Partnered with sales ops and marketing to fix a broken webinar lead handoff, routing sign-ups straight into the CRM with an assigned owner so reps followed up the same day instead of days later."
- "Worked with legal and finance to standardize our contract redline checklist, cutting back-and-forth email threads during quarter-end deal reviews."
- "Acted as the operational bridge between engineering and customer support during the [Feature/System] migration, translating known edge cases into a plain-language troubleshooting guide before launch."
- "Brought finance and product together to reconcile two conflicting churn definitions, documenting one shared calculation for quarterly board reporting."

### 2. Resolving trade-offs and disagreements

- "When design and engineering disagreed on the scope of the editor rebuild, I wrote a one-page comparison of both options with timeline trade-offs, helping the team agree on a phased rollout in a single meeting."
- "Flagged a scheduling conflict between two Q3 campaigns early and worked with both project leads to stagger launch dates so shared review resources weren't overloaded."
- "Helped stakeholders prioritize the top three export fixes ahead of the holiday freeze by sharing support ticket counts and customer call notes with the group."
- "When legal requested late copy changes two weeks before the pricing relaunch, I reorganized the review sequence with design so we got section-by-section sign-off and still launched on time."

### 3. Onboarding, mentoring, and knowledge sharing

- "Volunteered as onboarding buddy for two new teammates joining in the same month, pairing with them for an hour a day in week one and turning their questions into a permanent team FAQ."
- "Documented the step-by-step runbook for our monthly close / reporting process and walked a backup owner through two cycles so the team wasn't blocked when I took PTO."
- "Ran two 30-minute office-hour sessions for Customer Success to show them how to pull account usage filters themselves, freeing up both teams from manual data requests."
- "Shared a short monthly summary of top customer support themes with product and design so recurring setup friction got fixed at the source."

### 4. Supporting teammates under load

- "Stepped in to cover the [analytics backlog / vendor queue] for six weeks while we backfilled an open role, keeping weekly deliverables on time during the transition."
- "Reviewed specs and launch checklists for peers ahead of kickoff, catching a missing permissions step on the Q3 release before build started."
- "Created a shared intake form for ad-hoc requests coming into our team, protecting teammates' focus time and giving requesters clear turnaround expectations."
- "Joined two high-priority customer escalation calls with account management to help diagnose a configuration issue and document the fix for the wider team."

## Fill-in template for the collaboration section

```
Teamwork & Collaboration — [Review Period]

1. Cross-team handoff or alignment:
- Partnered with [Team A and Team B] on [project or workflow]. My part was [what you built, organized, or clarified], which meant [what got unblocked or stopped slipping].

2. Onboarding or knowledge sharing:
- Supported the team by [writing doc / onboarding new hire / training backup owner], so that [who benefits and how the team works better now].

3. Navigating trade-offs or escalations:
- When [project or request] hit [conflict, late change, or bottleneck], I worked with [stakeholders] to [action taken], allowing us to [outcome].
```

## Why collaboration wins are the first ones you forget

Your own big project has a folder or a ticket with your name on it. The hour you spent unblocking a colleague in April, the checklist you shared with legal in June, or the new hire you helped ramp in August leaves almost no trace by November.

If you don't write those moments down the week they happen, you end up defaulting to "I worked closely with the team" at review time.
