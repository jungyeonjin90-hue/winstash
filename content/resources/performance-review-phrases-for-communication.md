Almost every employee performance review evaluates **Communication Skills**.

And almost every review uses the same five exhausted phrases: *strong communicator, active listener, team player, keeps people updated, responsive in Slack.*

The problem is that these phrases describe activity rather than effectiveness. A manager doesn't care whether you typed 200 Slack messages today. They care whether **your communication prevented misunderstandings, sped up decisions, and kept projects moving without extra meetings.**

Below are before-and-after rewrites, 20 copy-and-paste performance review phrases across five communication competencies, and a fill-in template.

## Before and after: moving from generic traits to high-impact communication

| Weak (generic trait) | Strong (channel + behavior + outcome) |
| --- | --- |
| I communicate well with stakeholders. | Sent a concise one-page Friday status update to 15 cross-functional stakeholders, reducing ad-hoc check-in pings and eliminating our weekly status meeting. |
| I am responsive in team chat. | Maintained clear expectations by documenting shared decisions in project tickets rather than letting conclusions vanish into private Slack threads. |
| I present well in meetings. | Synthesized three months of user research into a five-slide readout with clear trade-offs, enabling leadership to agree on our Q4 roadmap in a single call. |
| I translate technical requirements for non-tech teams. | Wrote a plain-language troubleshooting guide with engineering that helped support reps resolve complex setup questions on day one without escalating. |

## 20 performance review phrases by communication competency

### 1. Written communication, documentation, and async clarity

- **Creating low-noise, consistent updates:** "I send a written weekly update every Friday covering what shipped, next priorities, and decisions needed—keeping stakeholders informed while respecting their time."
- **Writing clear, actionable project scopes:** "I move ambiguous requests into one-page project briefs that start with the problem, the success metric, and the trade-offs, which speeds up cross-team sign-offs."
- **Documenting decisions where people can find them:** "I make a habit of summarizing verbal and chat agreements in shared Jira tickets or project docs so teammates have an auditable source of truth."
- **Writing self-serve guides that reduce repetitive questions:** "Noticed the same five setup questions coming up weekly, authored a step-by-step FAQ in our team wiki, and cut routine clarification threads by half."

### 2. Cross-functional alignment and managing handoffs

- **Bridging context between technical and non-technical teams:** "I translate complex technical constraints into plain business trade-offs, helping non-technical partners understand timeline implications and make informed decisions."
- **Proactive risk and dependency communication:** "When a cross-team dependency slips, I flag it immediately with alternative options rather than waiting until the deadline passes."
- **Facilitating consensus across conflicting opinions:** "When design and engineering had differing approaches on [Project], I authored a comparison table with costs and timelines, facilitating agreement in a single meeting."
- **Establishing structured intake workflows:** "Replaced unstructured chat requests with a standardized intake form, setting clear turnaround expectations for partner teams."

### 3. Managing up and communicating with leadership

- **Bottom Line Up Front (BLUF):** "I lead executive briefings with the core decision, the trade-off, and my recommendation before diving into background details, keeping leadership discussions efficient."
- **No-surprise communications:** "My manager is never surprised by an escalating issue. I give early heads-up notices with a diagnosis and proposed solution before problems reach leadership."
- **Translating data into actionable next steps:** "Instead of presenting raw analytics tables, I structure reports around three core themes and specific recommendations for our roadmap."
- **Delivering constructive feedback upwards:** "I share honest, thoughtful feedback in retros and 1:1s, helping our team refine processes and avoid repeat bottlenecks."

### 4. Active listening, conflict resolution, and de-escalation

- **Calming frustrated stakeholders:** "When customer escalations or stakeholder tensions arise, I focus on acknowledging the friction, diagnosing the root cause, and setting realistic resolution milestones."
- **Active listening in requirements gathering:** "I ask clarifying questions to uncover the real underlying problem before proposing solutions, preventing unnecessary rework down the line."
- **Constructive, thoughtful code and document reviews:** "I frame spec and code feedback around scalability and edge-case principles rather than personal preference, keeping reviews constructive."
- **Making space for diverse perspectives:** "In group planning sessions, I intentionally invite input from newer or quieter team members before finalizing decisions."

### 5. Honest areas for improvement in communication

- **Brevity over detail:** "Earlier in the year, my proposal documents were sometimes too detailed, making them hard for busy stakeholders to skim. I have moved to a one-page summary format that puts the main recommendation on the first page."
- **Flagging blockers sooner:** "When tackling difficult problems in Q1, I occasionally spent days troubleshooting solo before raising a hand. I now follow a half-day rule: if I am stuck for more than four hours, I share what I've tried in our team channel."
- **Speaking up earlier in large cross-functional forums:** "In company-wide or large leadership meetings, I sometimes held back questions until 1:1 follow-ups. I'm focusing on bringing at least one prepared question to monthly readouts."
- **Setting async communication boundaries:** "I previously replied to Slack pings immediately at all hours, which fragmented my focus time. I now batch check-ins and use calendar focus blocks for deep project work."

## Fill-in template for communication competencies

```
Competency: Communication & Stakeholder Management — [Review Period]

1. Written Updates & Low-Noise Alignment:
- I keep stakeholders aligned through [weekly status update / project one-pager / FAQ], which [outcome, e.g., cut ad-hoc check-ins / sped up approvals].

2. Cross-Functional Translation & Collaboration:
- Partnered with [partner teams] by [action, e.g., mapping trade-offs / translating technical constraints], allowing the group to [deliverable or decision reached].

3. Managing Up & Executive Readouts:
- When presenting [project / data] to leadership, I [lead with recommendations / flag risks early], ensuring [clear decisions without surprises].

4. Area for Continued Growth:
- In the next period, I am refining [brevity / earlier risk escalation / public speaking reps] by [specific new habit].
```
