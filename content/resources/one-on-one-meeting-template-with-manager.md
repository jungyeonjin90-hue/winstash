Most weekly 1:1 meetings with a manager follow the same script: you spend twenty minutes reciting everything you worked on since last Tuesday, your manager nods, you run out of time with five minutes left, and neither of you talks about the actual blocker or your career goals.

Then November arrives, and neither of you can remember what happened in Q1 because nothing was written down.

A good **1:1 meeting template** fixes both problems at once. By keeping a single running document where you drop a short update and two or three talking points before the call, you get real decisions made in 25 minutes—and you quietly build your performance review record every week.

Below is a copy-and-paste 1:1 template, filled-in examples for normal weeks and career-check-in weeks, and questions you can pull from when you don't know what to ask.

## The running 1:1 meeting template (copy and paste)

Keep this in **one shared document** (Google Doc, Notion, or Confluence) with the newest date added at the top each week.

```
# [Your Name] / [Manager Name] — 1:1 Running Doc

Pinned: Current Quarter Goals
1. [Goal 1]
2. [Goal 2]
3. [Growth / next-level focus]

---

## [Date, e.g., Oct 27]

### 1. Quick context / shipped since last week (2-min skim, no need to read aloud)
- [Outcome shipped] — [what changed or link]
- [Progress on main project]

### 2. Decisions & trade-offs to discuss (10 mins)
- [Topic 1]: [Context in one line] -> My recommendation is [Option A] because [reason]. Agree?
- [Priority trade]: If [new urgent ask] comes in this week, I plan to push [Task B] to next week.

### 3. Blockers / air cover needed (5 mins)
- [What's stuck]: Waiting on [team/person] for [item] by [date]. Could use your help with [specific ask].

### 4. Feedback / growth (5-10 mins, rotate weekly)
- [One question about a recent project, skill, or next-level scope]

### Action items from today
- [ ] [You]: [Next step]
- [ ] [Manager]: [Decision or follow-up]
```

## Why this structure works for managers

1. **Current goals stay pinned at the very top.** Every time you and your manager open the doc, your quarterly priorities are right there at eye level.
2. **Wins are documented in writing first.** Your manager reads two bullets of what you finished in thirty seconds at the start of the call—and those lines stay in the doc forever for review season.
3. **You bring a recommendation, not just a problem.** Writing *"My recommendation is Option A—agree?"* cuts a fifteen-minute debate down to two minutes.

## Two filled-in 1:1 agenda examples

### Example 1: A normal execution week (Operations / PM)

```
## Oct 27

### 1. Quick context / shipped since last week
- Vendor approval intake sheet is live with finance and procurement; all new requests this week went through the form instead of email.
- Sent the Q4 kickoff checklist to the project team.

### 2. Decisions & trade-offs to discuss
- Legal asked for an extra compliance review step on the ERP rollout. To keep our Nov 15 launch date, I recommend moving the custom export view to a fast-follow release in late November. Does that trade-off work for you?

### 3. Blockers / air cover needed
- Still waiting on IT to grant read access for the two new analysts (ticket open 5 days). Could you ping the IT lead so their week-two training isn't blocked?

### 4. Feedback / growth
- None this week—let's use the extra 5 minutes on the Q4 roadmap trade-offs.

### Action items
- [ ] Me: Update ERP launch plan with phased export scope.
- [ ] Manager: Ping IT lead on analyst permissions today.
```

### Example 2: A monthly career & review check-in week

Once a month (and four weeks before formal reviews), use Section 4 to check your trajectory:

```
### 4. Feedback / growth (Monthly check-in)
- Looking at our Q3/Q4 goals pinned at the top, I've taken end-to-end ownership of the vendor intake overhaul and the new-hire onboarding doc.
- As we head toward review season, is there any gap between how I'm operating now and what you'd want to see for [Next Level / strong rating]?
- What is one thing I could take off your plate next month?
```

## 10 practical questions to ask when you have extra time

Skip generic icebreakers. Pick one question that actually helps you do your job better or clarifies how you're evaluated:

**On priorities and alignment:**
- "Looking at my list for the next two weeks, is there anything here that matters less than I think it does?"
- "What is the biggest thing leadership is worried about right now that our team should be aware of?"

**On feedback (specific, not vague):**
- "On the [Project X] rollout last week, was there one part of how I communicated or scoped it that you'd handle differently?"
- "Do you feel like you're getting the right level of detail in my [weekly status updates](/resources/weekly-status-update-template)—too much, too little, or about right?"

**On growth and promotion:**
- "Between now and the next review cycle, what would a 'clear yes' for [promotion / top rating] look like in this role?"
- "Where do you see me still relying on you where I could be making the call myself?"

## Turn your 1:1 notes into your self-evaluation

If you keep a running 1:1 doc, you will never stare at a blank self-evaluation form again. Every "Quick context / shipped" bullet you wrote at the top of your 1:1 agenda is a dated record of what you delivered, what trade-offs you navigated, and what blockers you cleared.
