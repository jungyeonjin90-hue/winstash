The last section of almost every self-evaluation form asks: "What are your goals for the next review period?" After spending hours writing about the past year, it's tempting to rush through this box with vague promises like "continue improving processes" or "collaborate better with stakeholders."

Vague goals hurt you twice. First, they look like filler today. Second, six or twelve months from now, your next self-review will grade you against whatever you wrote in this box—and it's hard to prove you achieved "better collaboration."

Good performance review goals are **concrete enough that six months from now, you and your manager can both point to what finished or changed.** Below is a simple structure and 18 copy-and-paste examples across common roles.

## The formula: deliverable, purpose, and checkable proof

A practical review goal answers three questions in one or two sentences:

1. **What will you build, fix, or own?** (The action or deliverable)
2. **Why does it matter to the team?** (The problem it solves)
3. **How will we know it's done?** (The date, milestone, or before-and-after change)

| Too vague to measure later | Specific and easy to prove at next review |
| --- | --- |
| Improve team documentation. | Document our top 5 recurring support workflows in the team wiki by the end of Q1 so new hires can handle them without escalating. |
| Communicate better with stakeholders. | Send a written weekly status update every Friday covering what shipped, next week's priorities, and blockers. |
| Be more strategic. | Run five customer interviews before Q2 planning and write a one-page recommendation on the top two workflow gaps to fix. |
| Help with new hires. | Create a first-week onboarding checklist and act as onboarding buddy for the two Q1 hires. |

## Performance review goals examples by category

Adapt the brackets and details below to match your team's actual roadmap.

### 1. Operations and process improvement goals

- "Consolidate vendor renewal dates and owners from scattered regional sheets into one master tracker by the end of [Month], and run a monthly check so we stop missing cancellation windows."
- "Build a standardized month-end close checklist with finance before [Q1/Q2] close, and train a backup owner so the close doesn't depend on one person."
- "Move internal requests from ad-hoc chat messages into a single intake form with clear triage rules by [Month], reducing back-and-forth on missing details."

### 2. Project and program management goals

- "Deliver the [Project Name] rollout on the agreed [Quarter] schedule by running weekly cross-team risk reviews and flagging dependency slips more than two days out."
- "Standardize our project kickoff template and post-launch retro format across the team in [Quarter], and use it on all new initiatives."
- "Establish a single weekly status update for [Program Name] stakeholders so leadership has a consistent view of progress, risks, and decisions needed."

### 3. Product management goals

- "Own the [Feature/Area] roadmap for [Half/Year]: run [N] customer discovery calls in Q1, define clear success criteria before build, and ship the core release in Q2."
- "Move all new product specs to a one-page format that starts with the problem, the trade-offs, and the success check, and review specs with engineering before kickoff."
- "Set up a monthly readout with support and sales to review top customer friction points and feed them into quarterly planning."

### 4. Customer success and account management goals

- "Redesign the onboarding checklist for new [Segment] accounts in Q1 to shorten the time from contract signature to first active workflow."
- "Run proactive quarterly check-ins with all [N] accounts up for renewal in the next half, identifying adoption gaps at least 60 days before renewal."
- "Partner with support to turn the top three recurring customer setup questions into self-serve help guides by the end of [Quarter]."

### 5. Cross-functional collaboration and leadership goals

- "Act as the onboarding buddy for new teammates joining in [Quarter] and keep our written context doc updated with their questions."
- "Lead the monthly cross-team sync between [Team A] and [Team B] to clear blocked requests and document shared decisions."
- "Delegate routine [weekly task/report] ownership by documenting the runbook and pairing with a teammate through two cycles in [Quarter]."

### 6. Personal work habits and growth goals

- "Keep a weekly log of completed work, decisions, and blockers every Friday so quarterly check-ins and next year's self-review are backed by concrete examples."
- "Share rough drafts of proposals within 48 hours to get early directional feedback instead of polishing in isolation."
- "Build working familiarity with [Tool/SQL/Analytics/Domain] by completing [specific project or internal analysis] in [Quarter]."

## Fill-in template for your review form

```
Goals for [Next Quarter / H1 / Year]

1. Core delivery / project goal:
- Own [project or workflow] and deliver [specific milestone] by [date/quarter] so that [what changes for users or the team].

2. Process / operational improvement goal:
- Replace [current messy process or bottleneck] with [checklist, intake form, or repeatable workflow] by [date], measured by [what stops breaking or slows down less].

3. Cross-team / leadership goal:
- Support the team by [onboarding new hires / documenting X / leading recurring sync], resulting in [concrete artifact or smoother handoff].

4. Personal habit / growth goal:
- Build the habit of [specific weekly or monthly practice] so that [how it helps your work or stakeholders].
```

## Don't let next year's goals disappear until next November

Here is what happens to most people after they submit their review: they close the tab, get busy with Monday's work, and don't look at their goals again for eleven months. Then the next review cycle starts, and they have to reconstruct a year of progress from memory all over again.

The easiest fix is to connect your goals to a five-minute Friday habit:

1. Copy the three or four goals you just wrote into the top of your [work brag sheet](/resources/brag-sheet-template-for-work).
2. Every Friday, jot down two or three lines about what you finished that week—or send a short [weekly status update](/resources/weekly-status-update-template) to your manager.
3. When next review season arrives, you already have a dated log of every milestone you hit—and if priorities changed mid-year, you have a record of when and why.
